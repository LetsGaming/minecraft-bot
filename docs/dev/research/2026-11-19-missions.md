# Missions: feasibility and reward research

Status: research only. Nothing is implemented. Written 2026-11-19.

The idea: daily, weekly and monthly missions. Each player gets a random mission from a pool, with a deadline and a reward when it is done. Missions must keep Vanilla balance and long-term play fun. A player who does not use the bot must not feel cheated. A player who uses it must feel rewarded.

## Verdict

**Conditional go, for a weekly-only MVP. Drop daily and monthly missions for now.**

- The code cost is moderate. About 70 percent of the parts exist (stats, scheduler, queue, payout, locales). Estimate for the MVP: 8 to 9 developer days, from reading the code, not from a measurement.
- The cost that decides this is content, not code. A mission pool needs about 15 good templates to stay fresh for weeks. Rewards need tuning. Both need upkeep.
- The bot's daily reward already creates streak pressure. Daily missions add a second chore with a second deadline. Weekly missions add much less pressure.
- Do not start before the checks in "Before building anything".

## Blind spots in the request

1. **No evidence of demand.** The bot records sessions (`player_sessions`, 365 days) and daily claims. Nobody has looked at how many linked players claim `/daily` each week. That number is the best forecast for mission use. If few players claim a free daily item, missions will not fix that.
2. **"Not using the bot" is a design input, not a footnote.** If missions need a Discord link, the players who do not link are the ones who feel left out. An in-game `!missions` command removes that gap. The in-game command framework already exists.
3. **The reward is the weak point.** Vanilla has few rewards that excite and do not break progression. The honest answer is cosmetics and time savers. If that is not exciting enough, no mission design will help.
4. **Stat based missions can be farmed.** Mob grinders, trading halls and craft loops fill counters without play. A pool of safe mission types is smaller than it looks.
5. **A possible false premise.** An earlier analysis in this project said Minecraft writes `stats/<uuid>.json` only on autosave or logout. That is **unverified**. The Minecraft Wiki does not say it. Do not design around it until tested.

## Before building anything

1. Count weekly `/daily` claimers per server from the claim store and `player_sessions`. Decide a threshold, for example "at least 30 percent of weekly active linked players".
2. Run the stats timing experiment: on the production version, watch the mtime and values of one player's stats file while the player mines, with no logout and no `save-all`. Then run `save-all flush` and compare. This sets how fresh progress can be.
3. Ask the players. A one question poll in Discord ("which would you do: weekly missions, more daily items, nothing") beats any search result.

Extras: daily and monthly periods add about 2 days of code but a large content cost. A dashboard editor for templates, with per-server exceptions like the rewards editor, adds 4 to 5 days. Opportunity cost: about two weeks of work that does not go to anything else on `docs/ROADMAP.md`.

### Technical risks

- **Progress lag.** The wrapper reads the stats file from disk. How often Minecraft writes it is unverified (see above). Use the difference from a stored baseline, accept lag, never promise live progress. A `save-all` before the final check could close the gap, at the cost of a console command per check.
- **Which clock rolls a period over.** Stats are per world, so missions are per server. Use `scheduleTimeZone(serverId)`, not the guild clock.
- **Name changes.** Key assignments on the UUID.
- **World resets.** Stats restart at zero. A baseline larger than the current value must cancel the mission, not give a negative progress.
- **Creative mode and some uses do not count** in vanilla stats (Minecraft Wiki, Statistics page). Mission text must not promise otherwise.
- **Modded servers.** Modded items appear as `modid:item` keys. Templates must tolerate a missing key.

## Mission types

Source for the stat keys and their limits: the Minecraft Wiki Statistics page. The exploit ratings are the author's judgment, not measured. L is low, M is medium, H is high.

| Type | Stat | Exploit risk | Advice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mine a hard to farm block | `mined:<block>` | M | Use deepslate ores, ancient debris, amethyst. Cap logs and crops at a low target. |
| Mine N different blocks | several `mined:` keys | L | Good: needs variety. |
| Craft an expensive item | `crafted:<item>` | H for cheap recipes | Craft and uncraft loops fill it. Use expensive items only. |
| Kill a rare mob | `killed:<mob>` | H for farmable mobs | Use phantom, witch, drowned, piglin brute. Or "N different mob types". |
| Travel on foot, by boat or swimming | `custom:walk_one_cm`, `sprint_one_cm`, `swim_one_cm`, `boat_one_cm` | M | Distances are in centimeters. Set targets that need a real trip. Leave out `aviate_one_cm` (elytra with fireworks loops). |
| Fish | `custom:fish_caught` | L to M for small N | AFK farms exist, so keep N small. |
| Breed animals | `custom:animals_bred` | M | Keep N small. |
| Trade with villagers | `custom:traded_with_villager` | H | A trading hall does it in a minute. Weekly at most, small N. |
| Earn an advancement | log line | L | Instant. The pool is finite per player: never assign one the player already has. |
| Play time | `custom:play_time` | H | Rewards idling. Do not use. At most a minimum gate. |
| Jumps, damage, deaths | various | H | Do not use. Rewarding or punishing deaths is perverse. |

Rules for the pool (author's judgment):

- Assign by what the player really does. Read the baseline. Do not assign a mission the player finishes on day 1.
- Scale the target from the player's own last week, with a cap, so new players and veterans both get a reachable goal.
- Allow one reroll per week. Unfinished missions lapse without a penalty.

## Reward balance

The rule: a reward must save time or add style. It must never skip a gate of progression. Everything a mission gives must also be obtainable in the world by a player who never touches the bot.

**Best fit: pay missions from the daily pool.** A mission reward is one draw from chosen tiers of the daily reward pool, using `drawFromPool` with a fixed tier chance table. The admin balances one pool in one place, the same items appear in `/daily`, and the tier system keeps the strong items rare.

| Mission period | Suggested draw | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | rare 70 %, epic 30 % | Time savers and cosmetics, a visible step above a daily draw. |
| Monthly (if ever) | epic 100 % | Memorable, not strong. |

- **Keep the legendary tier out of every mission reward.** The daily pool keeps progression skips (elytra, nether star, totem, conduit, dragon egg, heavy core, netherite upgrade template) in the legendary tier at about 2 percent per claim, by the owner's decision. That is luck. A guaranteed mission reward with a legendary chance would turn that luck into a grind payout.
- **Offer a choice of two or three rewards** when a mission completes (for example builder materials, a cosmetic, a time saver). A choice removes the frustration of a random draw, and costs little.
- **Do not pay**: netherite in any form, elytra, totems, mending books, beacon parts, shulker shells, diamond gear, enchanted golden apples, large diamond or emerald stacks (they feed villager trading and enchanting).
- **Scale**: a weekly reward is worth about 5 to 7 daily draws. These ratios are the author's suggestion, not sourced. Test them with the players.
- **No coupling to the daily streak.** Missions must not break, extend or boost it.
- **No public completion leaderboard.** It turns a bonus into a race.
- Delivery with `/give` drops items on the ground when the inventory is full. This behavior is from training knowledge and is not tested on the production version. Prefer delivery when the player is online, and check it once.

## Design rules from the literature

Evidence is summarised from the research pass. Most of the Minecraft specific part is author reasoning, because no Minecraft data was found.

- Engagement rewards are double edged. In a survey of 178 players the same reward felt like motivation, like fear of missing out, or like an obligation (Frommel and Mandryk 2022, abstract only).
- Streak resets read as punishment and some players quit. Mitigations that studios use: cumulative milestones, grace periods, recovery paths.
- Rerolls and skips are standard. Legends of Runeterra gives one free reroll a day, banked up to two. Battlefield gives weekly challenges three rerolls for everyone.
- Network servers (Hypixel) pay quests in their own XP and coins, not in vanilla loot. Faction quests there are capped at five per day and start automatically.
- Kill N and fetch N missions are the most common complaint in the sources found. The Minecraft specific part of that claim is inferred, not measured.

## What to change, in priority order

1. **Measure first.** Count weekly `/daily` claimers and run the stats timing experiment. Both take under an hour and can end the idea cheaply.
2. **Ship the queue fix and the tiered daily pool before any missions.** Missions depend on reliable delivery and on tiers for rewards.
3. **If the numbers support it, build the weekly MVP only:** one mission per player per server, about 15 templates, one reroll, no streaks, no penalty for a missed week, a choice of rewards drawn from rare and epic tiers.
4. **Make it reachable without Discord:** in-game `!missions` first, `/missions` second.
5. **Keep exploit prone types out of the first pool:** play time, craft loops, trades, farmable kills, `aviate_one_cm`.
6. **Add daily and monthly missions only after weekly engagement is proven.** A dashboard template editor comes last.
7. **If step 1 shows weak daily participation, drop missions** and spend the two weeks elsewhere.
